Exobiology without the guesswork

Know where to sample next, what grows on a planet before you land, and where the ×5 first-logged bonus may still be open.

On the surface: the sampling radar

  • Your previous samples around you, turned to the way you're facing, each with its colony-range circle.
  • A big readout: OK to sample or too close. Green means far enough.
  • Spoken alerts when a sample is logged, with the distance to move.
  • The compact HUD mode puts the radar on a second screen or the edge of your monitor.

Before you land: species predictions

From the body's atmosphere, gravity, temperature, volcanism and parent stars, Elite Nexus predicts which species should grow there and what they're worth. Checked against the developer's own sampling history, the top prediction for a genus was the species actually sampled 15 times out of 16. The first-logged bonus (×5) is shown when the body had no footfall, the system is uninhabited and the species wasn't logged.

Where to go next: two opposite strategies

  • Bonus hunt. Searches Spansh for planets where a valuable species should grow (Stratum Tectonicas by default) and nobody sharing data has a record of sampling it. It spells out how many planets suit the species, how many already have samples on record, which were mapped without the genus, which are in populated systems or were landed on, and which are left. Tour the best 15 plans the route.
  • Sure value. Spansh's exobiology planner: planets where commanders already logged valuable species. The payout is certain, but expect the base value; the bonus is most likely claimed.
Why "no record" isn't proof. Records come only from players whose tools share data through EDDN, and a sample leaves a record only when it adds a codex entry for that player. Treat a "no record" planet as a strong lead, not a guarantee. The further from the bubble, the more you'll find.
