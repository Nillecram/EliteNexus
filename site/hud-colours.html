Elite Dangerous HUD colours that keep hostile red

Recolour or dim the HUD with a live cockpit preview, start from community themes, and keep friend and foe colours where they belong.

The problem with HUD colour themes

Elite Dangerous recolours its HUD with a colour matrix: nine numbers in GraphicsConfigurationOverride.xml (the <MatrixRed>, <MatrixGreen> and <MatrixBlue> lines commanders share on forums). The matrix moves every colour at once. Turn the orange HUD blue and hostile contacts stop being red, friendly ones stop being green, and shields change too, which is the last thing you want in a fight.

What Elite Nexus does

  • Themes and a colour wheel. Start from the standard orange or a community theme (Mocha, Lemon, Empire blue and more, each credited to its author), then turn the colour wheel, lower the saturation, or lower the brightness to dim the whole HUD. The nine matrix numbers are there for exact themes.
  • A live cockpit preview and swatches showing what HUD orange, hostile red, friendly green, highlight blue and white turn into. It warns when hostile and friendly contacts would look alike, or the HUD gets too dark.
  • Keep hostile red, friendly green and shield blue. With Elite Nexus's optional graphics hook, the shaders that apply the game's matrix apply it only to the HUD's orange. Hostile contacts stay red, friendly ones green, and shields, other highlights and white keep their colours.
  • Share themes as the <MatrixRed> lines forums use, paste one someone shared, and save your own.
  • Safe edits. Applying writes only the three colour lines of the settings file and keeps everything else in it. Your original file is copied aside the first time, and a malformed file is never touched.

How to use it

  1. Download Elite Nexus, run it, and open HUD & cockpit › HUD colours.
  2. Pick a theme under Start from, adjust it, and check the preview and the warnings.
  3. Click Apply to Elite Dangerous. The game reads the file only when it starts, so quit to the desktop and start it again.
  4. To keep friend and foe colours, install the graphics hook from the Cockpit lights tab (switch its dimmer off there if you only want the colours) and turn on Keep hostile red, friendly green and shield blue.
What it can't do. The game has one matrix for the whole interface. It also tints station menus, the on-foot HUD and mission-board portraits, while the main menu stays orange, so no single element can be coloured on its own. The "keep hostile red" option needs the graphics hook, 3Dmigoto, an open-source Direct3D 11 hook that Elite Nexus downloads from its official release and checks against pinned checksums. It can't run alongside EDHM or another 3Dmigoto mod. Frontier tolerates mods like this without having approved them. Frontier documents none of the colour rules, so the thresholds that decide what counts as the HUD's orange can be adjusted under Options.

Coming from EDHM?

EDHM can colour single HUD elements, which Elite Nexus can't. Elite Nexus keeps to one theme for the HUD with friend-and-foe colours protected, plus a cockpit-light dimmer, and installs or removes everything in one click. The two can't be installed together; Elite Nexus warns when it finds EDHM.
