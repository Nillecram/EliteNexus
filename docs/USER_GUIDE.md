# Elite Nexus — user guide

Start `Start-EliteNexus.cmd` (or `dist\EliteNexus\EliteNexus.exe`). It finds your journal folder automatically
(Saved Games\Frontier Developments\Elite Dangerous), reads your whole history in well under a second, then follows
the game live. Run it before or after the game — order doesn't matter.

## The window

The **sidebar** on the left lists every page; on narrow windows it shrinks to icons (hover for names). At its foot
is your commander: name, current system and a fuel gauge (hover for ship and situation, click for the Dashboard).

Each page has a large title, and the **toolbar** at the top right shows, left to right: your credits, the live
market feed (**EDDN n/min**: click it for the market database), uploads sent this session (once you enable a service;
amber = a service is paused or rejecting, click for details), **Pause EDDN / Resume EDDN** when you share with EDDN
(market prices are only sent when you undock, so pausing while docked keeps a market you just found to yourself), and
**Live / Offline** (Live = the game is writing events). The round button next to it turns the window into a small
always-on-top **HUD** for a second monitor or the edge of your screen (location, fuel, next star and whether it's
scoopable, exobiology sampling radar, system value, last alerts). "Full view" switches back.

Alerts appear as banners at the top right and are spoken if you enable voice.

## Simple first, everything still there

Each page shows the one thing you usually need, with smart defaults (your current system, your ship's jump range and
landing pad). Every advanced setting the original tools offer, such as Spansh's routing algorithm, neutron/injection
choices or market filters, is one click away under **› Options**.

| HUD & cockpit | **HUD colours**: recolour or dim the game's HUD with its own colour setting (`GraphicsConfigurationOverride.xml`), with a live cockpit preview, and keep hostile red, friendly green and shield blue out of a theme. **Glare and exposure**: tame the glare of cockpit lights with the game's own tone-mapping values. **Cockpit lights**: dim the cockpit's own lights | **HUD colours**: **Start from** a theme (the standard orange or community themes such as Mocha, Lemon, Empire blue; hover one for its author and what to expect), then turn the **Colour** wheel, lower the **Saturation**, or lower the **Brightness** to dim the whole HUD. **Matrix** shows the nine numbers for exact themes. The swatches under the preview show what each standard colour becomes (HUD orange, hostile red, friendly green, highlight blue, white), and the page warns when hostile and friendly contacts would look alike or the HUD gets too dark. **Apply to Elite Dangerous** writes only the three colour lines of that file and keeps everything else in it; your original file is copied aside the first time. The game reads it only when it starts, so quit to the desktop and restart to see the change. **Copy as XML** and **Use a theme someone shared** take the `<MatrixRed>` lines commanders post on forums; **Save theme** keeps your own (right-click one to delete it). What it can't do: the game has one matrix for the whole interface (it also tints station menus, the on-foot HUD and mission-board portraits, while the main menu stays orange), so it can't colour one element on its own, and it can't dim or recolour the cockpit's lighting (the Cockpit lights tab dims it). If EDHM is installed, the page warns that EDHM needs this setting left at the standard orange. **Keep hostile red, friendly green and shield blue** (under In the game) answers the commonest complaint about themes, friend and foe changing colour with everything else: with Elite Nexus's graphics hook installed (the Cockpit lights tab installs it; switch its dimmer off there if you only want this), the HUD shaders that apply the game's matrix apply it only to the HUD's orange, so hostile contacts stay red, friendly ones green, and shields, other highlights and white keep their standard colours. The preview, the swatches and the warnings then show it that way. It's saved as soon as you flip it, and the game picks it up when it next starts. Whether a colour counts as the HUD's orange is decided by its green and blue as shares of its red; **Options** sets the three shares (most green 60 %, most blue 30 %, least green 3.5 %). Frontier documents none of this, so if hostile contacts still change colour or parts of the HUD stay orange, adjust them there. Without the hook the switch says what it needs, and it can't be used alongside EDHM or another 3Dmigoto mod. **Glare and exposure**: Odyssey's auto-exposure (brightness adaptation) reacts to the cockpit's own lights, so with a bright dashboard in view everything outside is drawn darker; it can't be switched off. The tab changes what players change instead, in the same file: **Glare from lights** (`GlareCompensation`; the game uses 1.33, lower means less glare around bright lights), the **Standard ACES curve** (draws darks and mid-tones brighter than the game's own curve, highlights the same; the page shows by how much) and, under **Auto-exposure targets**, the three `Percentiles` the exposure is set from. Presets: **Game default**, **Less glare** (glare 1.0) and **Morbad's Odyssey tweak** (CMDR Morbad's 2021 forum settings). Frontier doesn't document these values, so they are an experiment: they change the whole picture (space, stations, planets), the game reads them only at start, and **Game default** + Apply puts the game's own values back. The cockpit lamps themselves can't be dimmed this way; **Bloom** in the game's Graphics options sets how much bright lights glow. **Cockpit lights**: the game has no setting for its cockpit lights, so this tab installs a dimmer: 3Dmigoto (a Direct3D 11 hook placed next to the game; open source, GPL-3.0) plus a pattern that adjusts the game's light shader as the game loads it, so lights within the **Reach** of your eyes (about 5 m covers most cockpits) keep only the **Light kept** share of their light. Only the cockpit's own lights change: stations, carriers, planets and the HUD stay as they are. **Install the dimmer** first says exactly what happens, then downloads 3Dmigoto 1.4.9 (4.7 MB) from its official GitHub release, checks it against pinned checksums and puts d3d11.dll, d3dcompiler_47.dll, d3dx.ini, EliteNexus-3Dmigoto.json and a ShaderFixes folder next to EliteDangerous64.exe; **Remove** takes out exactly those files (any that changed since are left in place) and the files 3Dmigoto made. The same install is the graphics hook HUD colours uses to keep hostile red, friendly green and shield blue, so Remove switches that off too. Frame captures and exported shaders left in the game folder from setting 3Dmigoto up (`FrameAnalysis-…`, `ShaderCache`) are shown with their size, and **Delete them** removes them; the dimmer doesn't use them. Settings saved here apply the next time the game starts; in the game, **F9** switches the dimmer on and off, **F10** steps the light kept (0, 15, 35, 60, 100 %) and **F11** the reach (5, 8, 12 m), while the game window is in front. They work only without Shift, Ctrl or Alt held; if your active control presets use one of them on its own, the tab names the action, because pressing the key then does both. Elite Nexus never copies the game's code: the pattern finds the light shader by what its code looks like, so most game updates don't break it; if one does, the dimmer simply stops until Elite Nexus is updated. It can't run alongside EDHM or another 3Dmigoto mod (the tab says so if one is there), and Frontier tolerates such mods without having approved them. |

## Voice

Alerts are spoken through Windows speech. Choose the minimum priority in Settings:
* **Critical** — hull critical, ship destroyed.
* **High** — Earth-like/water/ammonia/terraformable/high-value bodies, motherlodes, analysis complete, massacre
  mission done, fuel low with a non-scoopable star next, interdiction.
* **Normal** — biological signals, sample 1 logged (+ distance to move), codex entries, carrier jump scheduled.
* **Low** — arrival announcements, discovery-scan body counts, system fully scanned.

## Data & privacy

Everything lives in `nexus-data\` next to the executable (settings, galaxy database, logs). Nothing is uploaded
unless you enable it in Accounts & sync; when you do, only live game data is sent (never your history, credits or
cargo), following each service's published rules.
