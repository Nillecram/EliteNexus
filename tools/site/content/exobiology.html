Live sampling radar

Stop guessing the distance to your next sample.

The radar follows your heading, so the top is the direction you are facing. Previous sample positions carry colony-range circles, and the page tells you how much farther to move.

  1. Take the first sample

    Your species, value, colony range and three-sample progress appear as you use the genetic sampler.

  2. Move beyond the colony range

    See the distance to your nearest sample and the remaining spacing. The radar keeps the previous sample positions in view as you turn and move.

  3. Look for “OK to sample”

    The indicator turns green once you are far enough from the recorded samples. Complete the analysis and the species joins your unsold data.

Know what to look for

The system view puts bodies with life together, with signal counts, gravity, temperature and atmosphere. Species predictions give you a lead before you commit to the surface.

From a prediction to an identification

FSS scans reveal the bodies and their biological signals. DSS mapping identifies the genera; Elite Nexus shows likely species, their values and sampling ranges. Predictions remain visibly distinct from species you have actually sampled.

Strategy one · Bonus hunt

Search for a chance at the first-logged payout.

Target a valuable species, with Stratum Tectonicas as the default, and find planets whose conditions suit it. The search separates the evidence so you can judge which leads are worth the trip.

No record

No shared record of mapping or sampling was found. The species is predicted, so you are choosing a promising candidate rather than a confirmed location.

Mapped, no samples

Mapping confirms the genus, but no sample is on record. The first-logged bonus may already be gone; these candidates are hidden by default.

Refine the search, then tour the best

Filter by evidence, predicted chance, distance from the arrival star and gravity. The page explains exclusions such as existing samples, mapping without the target genus, populated systems and known landings.

Tour the best 15 builds a nearest-first route through your selected prospects and hands it to Navigation.

A lead, with its evidence attached

A first-logged species can pay 5× its base value. But no shared record is not proof that nobody has visited: community coverage is incomplete, and not every sample creates a new Codex report. Elite Nexus keeps that distinction visible.

Strategy two · Sure value

Follow species that commanders have already found.

Prefer a route with known targets? The Spansh exobiology planner builds a tour of planets where valuable species are on record.

Choose your payout threshold

Set a minimum species value, search radius and number of stops. Review the systems, planets, recorded species and base values before following the route.

Expect the base payout on these trips: someone has already logged the species, so the first-logged bonus has most likely been claimed.

Same navigation, different strategy

Both known-species tours and bonus hunts go to Navigation. Arriving advances your progress, and automatic next-system copying makes it easy to keep moving.
